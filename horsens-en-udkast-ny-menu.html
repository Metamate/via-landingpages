Software Technology Engineering

Learn to develop software from the ground up: web applications, databases, cloud systems, embedded software and machine learning. Every semester you build a system with your project group, and in semester 5 you spend 20 weeks in a company. At the end, you can specialise in AI, data or interactive media.

Why study here?

An engineering degree

You graduate as a software engineer with a Bachelor of Engineering and can go on to a master's degree.

You learn by building

Every semester you work on a project in a group, often with a case from a company. The programme has an IoT lab and an XR lab where you put theory into practice.

Jobs in every industry

Only 4 % of recent software engineering graduates from VIA in Horsens are unemployed, and you can work as a software developer in almost any industry.

Internship guarantee

VIA offers an internship guarantee together with Horsens Business Alliance, a partnership between Horsens Municipality, local companies and business organisations. Hvad lover garantien konkret?

Your projects, semester by semester

Every semester you work on a large group project, and your courses give you the skills to build it.

  1. Screenshot from a project
    Semester 1

    Your first application

    You learn object-oriented programming from scratch, responsive web design and discrete mathematics. Your group designs, builds and documents an application for a single user.

  2. Screenshot from a project
    Semester 2

    A client/server system

    You learn to structure software so it can be tested and changed, and to store data in a database. Your group builds a system that several users can use at the same time.

  3. Photo from the lab
    Semester 3

    A distributed system

    You program close to the hardware in Assembler and C and build on that in Java and C#. You also learn algorithms and data structures. In the project, your group builds a system where parts written in different languages run on separate machines and communicate over the network.

  4. Screenshot of a web application with sensor data
    Semester 4

    An IoT system in the cloud

    In a larger team, you build a system that collects sensor data, uses machine learning to make predictions and shows the results in a web application. The system runs in the cloud, and you use continuous integration and delivery.

  5. Semester 5

    Work in a company for 20 weeks

    You work alongside the other employees, with an experienced colleague as your supervisor in the company and a supervisor from VIA. You find the place yourself, with help from VIA and the Career Centre, and you can also do your internship abroad. Companies don't have to pay interns, but many do (the engineers' union IDA recommends 16,200 DKK a month). If the internship is unpaid and you receive SU, you keep it.

    Students have interned in finance, energy, industry and consulting, for example. 3–4 navne på praktiksteder, hvis virksomhederne må nævnes

  6. Screenshot from a bachelor project
    Semesters 6–7

    Specialise and make your bachelor project

    You take a course in cyber security and work on a project in innovation and entrepreneurship. You choose six electives and can specialise by taking three in the same area. In semester 6 you can also go on exchange at one of our partner universities. In the last semester you make your bachelor project.

Choose your specialisation

A specialisation is three electives in semesters 6 and 7, and a large part of your bachelor project must be in the same area.

Machine Learning and AI Engineering

You learn to train and use models that learn from data, such as neural networks and generative AI, and the maths and statistics behind them. You can work as a machine learning engineer, data scientist or AI engineer, for example.

About the specialisation

Business Information Systems

You learn to collect, store and analyse large amounts of data so companies can make better decisions, using tools such as Power BI and SAP. You can work as a data engineer, BI developer or ERP developer, for example.

About the specialisation

Interactive Media

You develop user interfaces and applications for web, mobile and AR/VR, and make games in Unity. You can work as a frontend developer, full-stack developer or game developer, for example.

About the specialisation

Want to work with games and XR from your first semester? See Software Engineering – Games & XR in Viborg.

Is it for you?

Good to know before you apply

  • You don't need to know how to program. We start from the beginning.
  • You have maths in semester 1. If you choose AI or want to go on to a master's, there is more maths in the electives. If your maths is rusty, VIA has an online maths kickstarter course.
  • You work in a project group every semester, so you spend a lot of time working with others.
  • It's a full-time programme, around 40 hours a week.

Studying in Horsens

Students show you around Campus Horsens Video, approx. 90 sec. with subtitles

Campus Horsens is right next to the train station. 2,900 students on 27 programmes study here, and it is the centre of VIA's engineering programmes. Your class has 25–30 students from all over the world, including Danish students who want an international degree.

Study start introduces you to the programme, your class, the campus and Horsens. You meet your teachers and student counsellors, and an experienced student from the programme is your tutor and helps you settle in.

During the programme you visit companies, and former students come back to talk about their work. After hours there are Friday bars, parties and board games, and you can join social, academic and political student associations.

Your living costs depend on how you live, but including rent they are typically 5,000–7,000 DKK (about 650–900 EUR) a month. How to find housing · About Campus Horsens

After you graduate

Only 4 % of recent software engineering graduates from VIA in Horsens are unemployed. A typical starting salary is 42,500 DKK a month, and 58,200 DKK after 10 years. Source: Uddannelseszoom

Software engineers work in almost every industry, such as finance, defence, energy, manufacturing, healthcare, transport and consulting. You can work as a software developer, systems consultant, project manager or software quality engineer.

The Career Centre at Campus Horsens helps you with your CV, LinkedIn and job search in Denmark, and holds events where you meet companies. If you want to start your own company, VIA's student incubators give advice to students and graduates.

Go on to a master's degree

You can go on to a two-year master's degree. If you pass three electives in linear algebra, calculus and stochastic modelling, you meet the entry requirements for master's programmes at Aarhus University, such as Computer Engineering and Electrical Engineering. Admission is not guaranteed.

Questions and answers

Is it worth studying software engineering when AI can write code?
AI is part of the programme. You use AI tools in your courses and projects, and in semester 4 you have a course in machine learning and AI. Someone still has to design the software, decide what it should do and check that it works, and that is what you learn here.
I'm Danish. Can I apply?
Yes. The entry requirements are the same, and you apply through optagelse.dk as usual. The programme is also taught in Danish as Softwareingeniør (code 49319), so you can choose the class that suits you. See the Danish page
Do I need to speak Danish?
No. All teaching and exams are in English. Tilbyder VIA danskundervisning?
What does it cost?
The programme is free for citizens of the EU/EEA and Switzerland. Other applicants pay a tuition fee, and VIA does not offer scholarships. See tuition fees Remember to budget for rent and living costs. See what it costs to live in Horsens
Can I find an internship if I don't speak Danish?
Yes, but it can take more effort. Many software companies work in English, but not all of them. We work closely with companies in Horsens, have contacts all over Denmark, and the Career Centre helps you with your CV and applications. You can also do your internship abroad. If you want to stay in Denmark after you graduate, an internship here gives you a network and Danish work experience.
Can I stay and work in Denmark after I graduate?
Graduates from Danish programmes can apply for an establishment card, which gives you time to look for work in Denmark. tjek betingelser, link til nyidanmark.dk
Can I get the Danish state education grant (SU)?
Yes, if you are a Danish citizen. EU citizens can also qualify if they work in Denmark alongside their studies, for example 10–12 hours a week. SU for international students
I have a relevant degree already. Can I get credit transfer?
Yes, for example if you have an Academy Profession (AP) degree in computer science. You still have to meet the entry requirements.
Can I get support if I have a disability?
Yes, you can apply for special educational support (SPS). Contact VIA's SPS advisers

How to apply

  1. Check the entry requirements. You need an upper secondary qualification, such as a national school-leaving exam, the International Baccalaureate or the European Baccalaureate, that matches the Danish level of Mathematics A, English B and Physics B or Geoscience A. Each subject must be passed with an average of at least 2.0 on the Danish grading scale. If your exam is not Danish, see the extra requirements below. English proficiency requirements
  2. Apply at optagelse.dk by 15 March at 12:00 (CET). Use the programme code 49320. Upload your documents by 15 March, because they are part of the assessment. If you graduate in the summer, upload your final diploma by 5 July. Danish applicants can also apply in quota 1 by 5 July. If there are places left, you can apply until the programme starts.
  3. Send your signature page. If you don't have a Danish CPR number, sign the signature page from optagelse.dk and email it to apply@via.dk by 15 March at 12:00 (CET).
  4. You get an answer on 28 July. Remember to accept your place before the deadline.

If you are not an EU/EEA or Swiss citizen, you also pay an application fee and tuition, and you need a residence permit. The guide for non-EU/EEA applicants explains the extra steps.

Step-by-step guides: EU/EEA applicants · Non-EU/EEA applicants · Danish applicants

If your exam is not Danish
  • Your exam must include at least 375 hours of mathematics, 210 hours of English and 190 hours of physics or 375 hours of geoscience. The hours are 60 minutes each and must be from the last three years of upper secondary school.
  • Fill in the verification form to document the hours, and have your school stamp and sign it.
  • Upload a copy of your exam certificate and transcript of records, with a state-authorised translation of the transcript into Danish or English.
  • Check that your maths covers our maths syllabus.
  • If you don't meet the requirements but have other relevant qualifications, you can apply for admission with special permission.
How we assess applications

Applications by 15 March are assessed on your exam results, your grades in maths and English, relevant work experience and other documented qualifications, such as the IB, elite sport or volunteer work. If you have relevant work experience, upload a one-page CV and a declaration from your employer. You may be invited to an interview.

Admission rules and how to appeal

The programme only starts if enough students apply.
