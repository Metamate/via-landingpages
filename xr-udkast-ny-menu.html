Software Engineering – Games & XR

Skal titlen være "Software Technology Engineering" i stedet for "Software Engineering"? Kan vi udelade XR i undertitlen? Forslag: "Games & 3D".

Become a software engineer who builds interactive worlds. You learn software development from the ground up and specialise in game engines, 3D graphics, VR and mixed reality. Graduates work in game studios, XR and simulation companies, or as software developers in any industry.

Why study here?

An engineering degree

You graduate as a software engineer with a Bachelor of Engineering. Games and XR are how you learn, and the degree lets you work as a developer in any industry or go on to a master's.

Games, XR and 3D

From your first semester, games, graphics and XR are part of your timetable, alongside programming and software engineering. If you want the same degree without the specialisation, look at Software Engineering in Horsens.

A creative study environment

The programme works closely with The Animation Workshop, VIA's animation school, which is ranked third in the world in its field. You share the study environment with the animation students, do some projects together and make games in the student-run game club. You can also use TAW's motion capture equipment.

Your projects, semester by semester

Every semester you work on a large group project, and your courses give you the skills to build it. You work in Unity and Unreal Engine, and you also build your own game engine from scratch.

  1. Screenshot from a project
    Semester 1

    Code your first application

    You learn programming and object-oriented design from scratch, and your group builds a desktop application. You also start on game development and digital animation.

  2. Screenshot of a 2D game
    Semester 2

    Build a 2D game

    You learn to structure software so it can be tested and changed, and to store data in a database. Your group then designs and builds a 2D game that puts both to use.

  3. Photo of students wearing VR headsets
    Semester 3

    Create a VR experience

    You program 3D graphics in C++ and learn the linear algebra behind it. Your group builds an experience for VR headsets.

  4. Clip from a multiplayer game
    Semester 4

    Build an online multiplayer game

    Your group builds a game in Unreal Engine that several players can play together over a network. You learn about networks and distributed systems, and how to test code, including code written by AI.

  5. Semester 5

    Work in a company for 20 weeks

    You join a game studio, a software company or another workplace, in Denmark or abroad. You find the place yourself, with help from VIA and the Career Centre, and a supervisor from VIA follows you. Many companies pay interns (the engineers' union IDA recommends 16,200 DKK a month). If the internship is unpaid and you receive SU, you keep it. Meet Kate and Astrid, who interned at AquaGlobe

    Students have interned in game studios, software companies and companies making e-learning and interactive products. 3–4 navne på praktiksteder, gerne en blanding af spilstudie, softwarevirksomhed og simulering eller XR, hvis virksomhederne må nævnes

  6. Screenshot from a bachelor project
    Semesters 6–7

    Choose your electives and make your bachelor project

    You learn algorithms, computer systems, cyber security and user experience, and work on an innovation project. In semester 6 you can also go on exchange at one of our partner universities, with help from the programme's international counsellor. In the last semester you choose three electives and make your bachelor project.

Is it for you?

Good to know before you apply

  • You don't need to know how to program. We start from the beginning.
  • There are 15 ECTS of maths, and you use it for 3D graphics and games. If your maths is rusty, VIA has an online maths kickstarter course.
  • This is an engineering degree, not an art degree. You take one course in animation, but if you want to draw and animate, The Animation Workshop is the right place.
  • It's a full-time programme, around 40 hours a week.

Studying in Viborg

Students show you around Video, approx. 90 sec. with subtitles

You study in a class of around 25 students. During the programme you visit companies, and former students come back to talk about their work.

Study start introduces you to the programme, your class, the campus and Viborg. You meet your teachers and student counsellors, and tutors help you settle in. The programme is in old military barracks shared with The Animation Workshop. After hours there are game jams, clubs and Friday bars. Students from the programme and TAW meet every week in a game club to make games, and the club has taken part in Nordic Game Jam.

Viborg is a town of about 40,000 people in the middle of Jutland, about an hour from Aarhus, and a UNESCO Creative City of Media Arts. Every year Viborg hosts Viborg Animation Festival (VAF). The festival draws the animation and games industry, and students show work from their projects there.

Your total living costs, including rent, are typically 6,500–7,500 DKK (about 875–1,000 EUR) a month. VIA recommends Camp Logos, a student residence right next to campus, where both Danish and international students live. You contact Camp Logos directly to get a room. How to find housing in Viborg

Anbefaling: Vis en almindelig dag med lokalerne, gruppen og en underviser, ikke et interview foran en grøn skærm. Lad gerne både en dansk og en international studerende fortælle, og tag klip fra Viborg Animation Festival (VAF) med.

After you graduate

Only 4 % of recent software engineering graduates from VIA in Horsens are unemployed. A typical starting salary is 42,500 DKK a month. Source: Uddannelseszoom

VIA's Career Centres help you with your CV, LinkedIn and job search, and hold events where you meet companies. If you want to start your own studio or company, VIA's student incubators give advice to students and graduates.

Go on to a master's degree

You can go on to a two-year master's degree. If you pass three electives in linear algebra, calculus and stochastic modelling, you meet the entry requirements for master's programmes at Aarhus University, such as Computer Engineering and Electrical Engineering. Admission is not guaranteed.

Questions and answers

Is it worth studying software engineering when AI can write code?
AI is part of the programme. You use AI tools in your courses and projects, and in semester 4 you have a course in AI-assisted development and testing. Someone still has to design the software, decide what it should do and check that it works, and that is what you learn here.
I'm Danish. Can I apply?
Yes. The entry requirements are the same, and you apply through optagelse.dk as usual. If you don't have an upper secondary exam, you can take VIA's admission course for engineering programmes. All teaching is in English, so you should be comfortable reading and writing English.
Do I need to speak Danish?
No. All teaching and exams are in English. Tilbyder VIA danskundervisning?
What does it cost?
The programme is free for citizens of the EU/EEA and Switzerland. Other applicants pay a tuition fee, and VIA does not offer scholarships. See tuition fees Remember to budget for rent and living costs. See what it costs to live in Viborg
Can I find an internship if I don't speak Danish?
Yes, but it can take more effort. Many game and software companies work in English, but not all of them. We help you find a place through our network, and you can also do your internship abroad. If you want to stay in Denmark after you graduate, an internship here gives you a network and Danish work experience.
Can I stay and work in Denmark after I graduate?
Graduates from Danish programmes can apply for an establishment card, which gives you time to look for work in Denmark. tjek betingelser, link til nyidanmark.dk
Can I get the Danish state education grant (SU)?
Yes, if you are a Danish citizen. EU citizens can also qualify if they work in Denmark alongside their studies, for example 10–12 hours a week. SU for international students
I have a relevant degree already. Can I get credit transfer?
Yes, for example if you have an Academy Profession (AP) degree in computer science. You still have to meet the entry requirements.
Can I get support if I have a disability?
Yes, you can apply for special educational support (SPS). Contact VIA's SPS advisers

How to apply

  1. Check the entry requirements. You need an upper secondary qualification, such as a national school-leaving exam, the International Baccalaureate or the European Baccalaureate, that matches the Danish level of Mathematics A, English B and Physics B or Geoscience A. Each subject must be passed with an average of at least 2.0 on the Danish grading scale. If your exam is not Danish, see the extra requirements below. English proficiency requirements
  2. Apply at optagelse.dk by 15 March at 12:00 (CET). Choose "Diplomingeniør i softwareteknologi" (the Danish name of the programme). koden er ikke offentliggjort endnu Upload your documents by 15 March, because they are part of the assessment. If you graduate in the summer, upload your final diploma by 5 July. Danish applicants can also apply in quota 1 by 5 July. If there are places left, you can apply until the programme starts.
  3. Send your signature page. If you don't have a Danish CPR number, sign the signature page from optagelse.dk and email it to apply@via.dk by 15 March at 12:00 (CET).
  4. You get an answer on 28 July. Remember to accept your place before the deadline.

If you are not an EU/EEA or Swiss citizen, you also pay an application fee and tuition, and you need a residence permit. The guide for non-EU/EEA applicants explains the extra steps.

Step-by-step guides: EU/EEA applicants · Non-EU/EEA applicants · Danish applicants

If your exam is not Danish
  • Your exam must include at least 375 hours of mathematics, 210 hours of English and 190 hours of physics or 375 hours of geoscience. The hours are 60 minutes each and must be from the last three years of upper secondary school.
  • Fill in the verification form to document the hours, and have your school stamp and sign it.
  • Upload a copy of your exam certificate and transcript of records, with a state-authorised translation of the transcript into Danish or English.
  • Check that your maths covers our maths syllabus.
  • If you don't meet the requirements but have other relevant qualifications, you can apply for admission with special permission.
How we assess applications

Applications by 15 March are assessed on your exam results, your grades in maths and English, relevant work experience and other documented qualifications, such as the IB, elite sport or volunteer work. If you have relevant work experience, upload a one-page CV and a declaration from your employer. You may be invited to an interview.
